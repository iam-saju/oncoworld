# Gold answer: intracranial mesenchymal tumor journey, step L2 (molecular-access)

Status: draft, pending oncologist sign-off.

## q1 - next investigation

Comprehensive molecular/genomic profiling of the tumor: broad DNA/RNA next-generation sequencing with fusion
detection on the available tissue, since any therapeutic option beyond conventional chemotherapy depends on
finding an actionable alteration. The final histologic entity is still not established, so this also continues
the diagnostic workup, not just a treatment-selection step.

## q2 - tissue inadequate for molecular testing

If the archival resection tissue is insufficient in quantity or quality for the full panel, first request
additional sections or remaining block material from pathology, and ask the molecular laboratory whether a
smaller, targeted panel can be run on the limited material. If that is still inadequate, consider a new
image-guided biopsy of accessible residual or recurrent tumor, weighing the procedural risk against the value of
the result, and discuss this explicitly with the patient and family before proceeding.

## q3 - family declines repeat brain biopsy

Respect the family's decision; do not press for a repeat brain biopsy. Because there is already imaging evidence
of a C6 pial nodule suspicious for spinal drop/leptomeningeal dissemination, cerebrospinal fluid is a reasonable,
less invasive alternative source of tumor material: send CSF for cytology and CSF-based molecular testing
(cell-free/circulating tumor DNA), in addition to routine CSF studies, via lumbar puncture.

## q4 - CSF-based testing also not feasible

If the CSF sample is not adequate for molecular extraction, fall back to a peripheral blood (plasma) liquid
biopsy for circulating tumor DNA. Explain to the family that blood-based testing has lower sensitivity for
disease confined to the central nervous system than CSF or tissue, so a negative result would not rule out an
actionable alteration, but it is the least invasive remaining option and can still be informative, particularly
given the documented leptomeningeal/spinal involvement that may shed tumor DNA systemically.
