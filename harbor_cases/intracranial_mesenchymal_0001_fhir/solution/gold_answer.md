# Gold answer: intracranial mesenchymal tumor, initial tumor-board plan

Status: draft, pending oncologist sign-off.

## Initial assessment

A symptomatic left frontal extra-axial/cortically based intracranial mesenchymal neoplasm with a separate
enhancing C6 pial nodule suspicious for spinal drop or leptomeningeal dissemination. "Intracranial mesenchymal
tumor" is not a specific diagnosis: the entity and WHO grade are not established, so it cannot by itself
determine therapy. Specific candidates worth naming, without favoring one over the others on this information,
include solitary fibrous tumor/hemangiopericytoma and a sarcoma such as Ewing sarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, or
metastatic sarcoma.

## Diagnostic confirmation and staging

Expert neuropathology review with expanded immunohistochemistry and broad DNA/RNA sequencing, including fusion
detection. If solitary fibrous tumor is in the differential, confirm STAT6 and NAB2-STAT6. A molecular result
alone is not sufficient to establish the entity or to select targeted therapy; it must be interpreted with the
histology and confirmed. Complete contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain and entire spine, characterize the C6
nodule as suspected spinal drop/leptomeningeal disease, and obtain systemic staging (CT chest/abdomen/pelvis
or PET-CT) for extracranial disease as clinically appropriate. Do not assign a formal stage or
treatment-specific grade from the available wording alone.

## Immediate management plan

Do not start disease-directed chemotherapy or targeted therapy on the current information. Defer definitive
treatment until the diagnosis, WHO grade, extent, and resectability are established. Refer urgently to a
multidisciplinary neuro-oncology tumor board. Neurosurgery should assess maximal safe resection; when feasible,
gross-total resection is the goal because surgery provides tissue and local control. Provide symptom control
for headache and vomiting, assess for raised intracranial pressure and seizures, and treat those complications.

Once the diagnosis and grade are established, a high-grade solitary fibrous tumor or a subtotal resection would
warrant postoperative focal radiotherapy to the surgical bed or residual disease. Routine adjuvant chemotherapy
is not the default after complete resection of localized disease, and systemic therapy is reserved for
unresectable, metastatic, or recurrent disease. The evidence for solitary fibrous tumor/hemangiopericytoma is
largely retrospective, so do not present any regimen as a universal Category 1 standard. Arrange long-term
serial contrast-enhanced MRI surveillance, with extracranial surveillance as clinically appropriate.

## Prognosis

Cannot be meaningfully stated until the entity and WHO grade are established: outcomes for intracranial
mesenchymal/sarcomatous neoplasms vary widely by the specific diagnosis, grade, extent of resection, and whether
there is craniospinal dissemination. A confirmed high-grade sarcoma, or one with leptomeningeal/spinal
dissemination as suspected here, would carry a substantially worse prognosis than a lower-grade, resectable
lesion without craniospinal spread. Do not quote a specific survival figure before the diagnosis is confirmed.

## Missing information

- Complete contrast-enhanced brain and spine MRI
- Extent of resection or resectability assessment
- Systemic staging for extracranial disease
